## Section 2 — Direct answer

_Quotable standing alone — see `geo.md`._

Back-office operations covers the recurring administrative work of employing people:
running payroll and remitting to the CRA, administering the group benefits plan,
processing joiners, movers and leavers across every system, and keeping the records in a
state that survives an audit. For most Ontario businesses under 150 people it's between
half a day and two days a week of someone's time, and it's almost never that person's
actual job.

## Section 3 — The problem

**Eyebrow:** What goes wrong

**H2:** It's not difficult. It's just never finished.

**Lead:**
Every one of these tasks is manageable in isolation. What makes them expensive is that
they arrive continuously, they can't be deferred, and getting one wrong is disproportionately
costly.

**Cards:**

**The deadlines aren't yours to move.**
CRA remittance dates, benefits enrolment windows, ROE timelines. Miss one and the penalty
is out of proportion to the effort of not missing it.

**Onboarding is four systems and a memory.**
HRIS, payroll, benefits, and every application they need access to. When it's held in
somebody's head, the day someone forgets is the day a new hire sits idle.

**Offboarding is the one that actually costs you.**
Final pay and the ROE get done because someone chases them. Revoking access across
eleven systems doesn't, because nobody's chasing it. Ghost accounts are the most common
security finding we make on a new account.

**The systems disagree.**
Payroll thinks she's part-time, the HRIS thinks she's full-time, benefits has her on the
old plan. Every mismatch surfaces eventually, usually as a deduction error someone has to
explain.

## Section 4 — What we do

**Eyebrow:** What we do

**H2:** Five running jobs, on a schedule.

**01 · Joiner, mover, leaver**
The flagship, and the one that ties this pillar to the software stack. When someone
starts, their accounts, licences and permissions are provisioned before day one. When they
change roles, permissions change with them rather than accumulating. When they leave,
access is revoked everywhere the same day, the device comes back, the licence is
reclaimed, and the ROE goes out on time. One workflow, HR and IT ends both.

**02 · Payroll**
Pay runs, direct deposits, CRA remittances, T4s, ROEs, and the year-end. Run to schedule,
with the documentation kept in a state you could hand to an auditor without a week of
preparation.

**03 · Benefits administration**
Enrolments, life events, terminations, and keeping the payroll deductions synchronised
with what the plan actually says. Your broker designs and sells the plan; we run the daily
administration of it, and answer the employee questions so they don't come to you.

**04 · HR operations**
The HRIS itself — employee records, time off, policy acknowledgements, document
retention. Configured properly once, then maintained, so the answer to "where is that
signed?" takes seconds.

**05 · Bookkeeping**
Day-to-day transaction coding, reconciliations, payables and receivables, kept current
so your accountant is doing accounting rather than cleanup at year-end.

## Section 5 — Scope

**Eyebrow:** What we don't do

**H2:** We run it. We don't advise on it.

**Lead:**
Worth being exact about, because the distinction gets blurred in this market and the
blurring is usually deliberate.

**Body:**
We administer systems and process. We are not your lawyer, your accountant, or your
benefits broker, and we don't replace them. Tax positions stay with your CPA. Employment
law questions stay with counsel. Plan design stays with your licensed advisor. Clinical
decisions stay with your clinicians.

You remain the employer of record throughout — this is not a PEO arrangement and we don't
take on that liability. What we take on is the running of it, which is the part actually
consuming your week.

**Honest limit:**
Under about five employees this rarely pays for itself; the admin isn't yet heavy enough
to justify handing over. We'll tell you if we think you're there, and we'd rather set you
up properly and revisit in a year.

## Section 6 — FAQ

**H2:** Questions we get about this.

**Is this fractional HR?**
No. A fractional HR consultant gives you advice and a plan, and you implement it.
We do the running: the pay runs go out, the enrolments get processed, the accounts get
provisioned. If you need someone to design a compensation philosophy, that's a different
kind of help and we'll point you at it.

**Do you replace our bookkeeper or office manager?**
Usually not — more often we take the pieces they dread and give them their week back.
Where a role genuinely overlaps, we'll say so rather than quietly duplicating it. The
comparison worth doing is against what it costs to hire the coverage, and that's on the
[pricing page](/pricing).

**Who's the employer of record?**
You are. We administer; we don't take on employment liability or become a co-employer.
That's a deliberate choice — the arrangements that do take it on cost considerably more
and constrain how you hire.

**How does this connect to the software side?**
It's the same workflow seen from two ends. When HR processes a new hire, the accounts,
licences and permissions provision automatically. When HR processes a termination, access
revokes across every system the same day. Companies that split these between two vendors
are the ones with ghost accounts, and they usually don't know it.

**What payroll software do you use?**
Whatever you're already on, if it's sound — we'd rather not move you. Where you're
choosing from scratch or the current system is fighting you, we'll recommend based on
your size, complexity and province mix, and handle the migration.

**Do you handle Ontario-specific compliance?**
We keep records and processes aligned with ESA requirements, WSIB reporting and CRA
remittance obligations, and we flag when something's changed. We don't provide legal
opinions or guarantee compliance — that's your counsel's role, and anyone promising
otherwise is overselling.

**Can government funding cover this?**
The systems work often qualifies. DMAP covers up to $15,000 of planning for eligible
Ontario for-profit SMEs, and TDP up to $50,000 of implementation after a completed DMAP.
Ongoing administration isn't grant-funded. We'll tell you which parts of your project
plausibly qualify before you apply. [More on funding →](/funding)
