## Section 2 — Direct answer

_First body paragraph. Written to be quotable standing alone — see `geo.md`._

Business software management means keeping an accurate picture of every application a
company pays for, who actually uses it, how the pieces connect, and what happens when one
of them stops working. For a fifty-person company it usually means reclaiming somewhere
between a tenth and a third of the software budget in the first year, and cutting the time
a new hire waits for access from days to under an hour.

## Section 3 — The problem

**Eyebrow:** What goes wrong

**H2:** Software decisions get made once, then never revisited.

**Lead:**
Every tool in your stack was a reasonable choice on the day it was chosen. The trouble is
that nobody's job is to look at all of them together, and the cost of that shows up
slowly.

**Cards:**

**Nobody owns the list.**
Licences for people who left. Tools that lost their champion. A handful of apps someone
expensed and never mentioned. The invoice keeps clearing, so nothing prompts a review.

**The pieces don't talk.**
Your CRM doesn't know what your accounting software knows. Someone bridges the gap by
retyping it, every week, forever.

**Access is a favour, not a process.**
New hires wait on a chain of forwarded emails. Leavers keep their logins for months.
Both are the same missing process, and only one of them is visible.

**When something breaks, you triage it.**
Four vendors, four support portals, four sets of hold music, and you're the one deciding
which of them owns the problem.

## Section 4 — What we do

**Eyebrow:** What we do

**H2:** Four things, in the order they matter.

**Lead:**
This is deliberately sequential. Integrating tools you shouldn't be paying for, or putting
AI on top of data nobody has organised, is work that has to be undone later.

**01 · Choose**
We look at how your team actually works before we look at what's available. Often the
recommendation is to keep what you have and use more of it — a licence you already pay for
usually beats a new subscription. Where something genuinely needs replacing, we say so and
explain what it costs to move.

**02 · Implement and integrate**
We do the migration, the configuration, and the connections between systems. The measure
of success is that nobody is retyping data from one screen into another.

**03 · AI where it earns its place**
Some of this is real and some of it isn't. We put AI where there's a repeated task with a
clear input and a checkable output, and we get the permissions right first, because a
tool that can summarise your files can also surface the ones the wrong person shouldn't
see. Where AI wouldn't help, we'll tell you.

**04 · Run it**
Identity and lifecycle, licence tuning, endpoint security on every device, and the
day-to-day support your team leans on. On the managed tier this is continuous rather
than a project.

## Section 5 — The support model

**Eyebrow:** When something breaks

**H2:** One number, every vendor.

**Lead:**
This is the part most people underestimate until they've lived without it. You don't call
the software vendor, and you don't work out which of your four vendors owns the problem.
You call us.

**Body:**
We're your first line. Most things get resolved without going any further — access,
configuration, the thing that worked yesterday. What doesn't, we escalate on your behalf,
to the vendor, in their language, with the diagnostics they need, and we chase it. You
find out when it's fixed.

What that's worth is not really the fixing. It's that nobody on your team spends an
afternoon establishing whose fault something is.

**Honest limit:**
We're not a break-fix shop and we don't do desk-side hardware repair. If a laptop's screen
is broken we'll manage the warranty claim and get a replacement enrolled, but somebody
local is physically handling it.

## Section 7 — FAQ

**H2:** Questions we get about this.

**Is this the same as hiring an MSP?**
No, and the difference is what we're accountable for. An MSP keeps infrastructure running
and bills against tickets. We choose and run the software your business operates on,
integrate it, and act as the single point of contact across every vendor in the stack —
including the ones an MSP wouldn't touch, like your HR system or your accounting software.

**What happens when we already have an IT provider?**
That's common and it works fine. We handle software operations, identity, licensing and
vendor coordination; they keep doing whatever they do well. We'd rather coordinate with
them than replace them, and we'll say so honestly if we think the overlap isn't worth
paying for twice.

**How quickly do you answer when something breaks?**
Business hours on the Foundations tier, with continuous monitoring and vendor escalation
on Managed. Response targets are on the [pricing page](/pricing) — we publish them rather
than negotiate them per client.

**Will you actually recommend we buy less software?**
Frequently. The first pass on a new account usually finds duplicate tools and licences
nobody's opened in months, and cutting those often covers a meaningful share of what we
cost. That's a better first month than selling you something.

**Is AI enablement just Copilot licences?**
Copilot is often part of it, but buying the licence is the easy step and not the one that
makes it work. The work is fixing file permissions so it doesn't surface things it
shouldn't, connecting it to the systems where your actual data lives, and showing people
the handful of tasks it genuinely does better. Where none of that adds up, we'll say the
licence isn't worth buying.

**Can government funding cover this?**
Often, for eligible Ontario for-profit SMEs. DMAP covers up to $15,000 of planning work,
and TDP up to $50,000 of implementation for businesses that have completed a DMAP and
clear a $750,000 revenue floor. We'll tell you before you apply whether you're likely to
qualify. Approval and amounts are decided by OCI, not us. [More on funding →](/funding)
