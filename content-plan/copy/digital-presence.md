## Section 2 — Direct answer

_First body paragraph. Quotable standing alone — see `geo.md`._

Managing a digital presence means keeping four things in step: a website that loads fast
and answers the question, search and answer-engine visibility for the terms buyers
actually use, paid campaigns steered against results rather than set and left, and
accurate business listings wherever people check. Most small businesses have all four,
owned by nobody, drifting apart at different speeds.

---

## Section 3 — The problem

**Eyebrow:** What goes wrong

**H2:** Ads are the easy part.

**Lead:**
The hard part is everything after the click — whether the site loads, whether the form
goes anywhere, whether anyone answers. Spending more at the top of that doesn't fix any
of it.

**Cards:**

**The destination is slower than the campaign.**
A site built on a heavy theme takes four seconds on a phone, which is where most local
searches happen. You paid for the click either way.

**The form goes to an inbox nobody owns.**
Leads arrive, sit, and go cold. This is the most common and least visible failure we find,
and it is not a marketing problem.

**The listings disagree with each other.**
Different hours on Google than on Apple Maps, an old address on Bing, a phone number
that rings a line you stopped using. Each one quietly costs you.

**Nobody's steered it since launch.**
Campaigns get built well and then left. Twelve months later the budget is going to
keywords that stopped converting in month three.

## Section 4 — What we do

**Eyebrow:** What we do

**H2:** Four surfaces, kept in step.

**01 · The website**
Built for speed and clarity rather than for a portfolio screenshot. Semantic markup, real
accessibility, and forms that go somewhere specific — into your CRM, to a named person,
with a follow-up that fires whether or not anyone's at a desk. Managed on our
infrastructure, or handed over on open standards if you'd rather own it.

**02 · Search, and answer engines**
Technical foundations first: structure, schema, crawlability, Core Web Vitals. Then the
content that answers what people actually ask. Increasingly the query happens in a chat
window rather than a search box, and the work of being the source it quotes is different
from ranking — we do both, and we're explicit about which one a given change serves.

**03 · Paid campaigns**
Google Search, Local Services Ads, and paid social where the audience is genuinely there.
Steered monthly against booked work, not impressions. Small budgets are fine and often
better — a tight local campaign beats a broad one at the same spend.

**04 · Social and listings**
Enough social presence that you look active when someone checks, without pretending it's a
content operation. Google, Bing and Apple listings verified and consistent, because that's
what a buyer looks at last, right before deciding.

## Section 5 — The sequence

**Eyebrow:** The order we'd do it in

**H2:** We'd rather fix the destination first.

**Lead:**
This is where we differ from an agency, and it costs us revenue in the first month, so
it's worth explaining.

**Body:**
More traffic into a business that can't handle the leads it already has makes things
worse, not better. If the site is slow, or the enquiry lands in an inbox nobody watches,
or the person who'd follow up is the same person on a roof, then the campaign is
converting your money into frustration efficiently.

So we look at the destination and the follow-up first. Often that's a small piece of work
and we turn campaigns on the same month. Sometimes it means we tell you not to spend on
ads yet, which is a strange thing for the party being paid to run them to say, and is
exactly why we say it.

**Honest limit:**
We're not built for enterprise budgets or brand campaigns. If you need a national creative
strategy, we're the wrong shop. If you need to be the obvious choice within an hour's
drive, we're well set up for that.

## Section 6 — FAQ

**H2:** Questions we get about this.

**Do we have to buy the website to get the campaigns?**
No. If your site is sound we'll say so and spend the budget on demand instead. We'll
audit it either way before we recommend anything — that part's free and you keep the
findings.

**How is this different from hiring a marketing agency?**
An agency is measured on what it can control, which is usually clicks. We also run the
systems the lead lands in, so we can follow it all the way to a booked job and tell you
which campaigns produced revenue rather than traffic. It also means when the form breaks,
it's ours to fix.

**What is GEO, and does it matter yet?**
It's optimising to be the source an AI assistant quotes when someone asks it a question,
rather than a blue link they click. It matters now for research-heavy and comparison
queries — the ones where a buyer asks "who should I use for X in Ottawa" — and the work
is different: structured facts, dated claims, direct answers, clean schema. We build it
in rather than selling it separately.

**Do you lock us into a contract?**
Month to month with 30 days' notice, on both services. Website plans carry a one-time
setup cost; there's no minimum term after it. Numbers are on the [pricing page](/pricing).

**Can you work with the site we already have?**
Usually. We'll tell you honestly when a rebuild is cheaper than the accumulated fixes —
that's normally true of older page-builder sites where performance can't be recovered
without pulling the theme out.

**Will you handle our Google Business Profile?**
Yes, along with Bing and Apple. It's unglamorous and it's frequently the highest-return
thing we do in the first month, particularly for anyone with a service area rather than a
storefront.
