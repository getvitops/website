## Section 3 — Why the company exists

**Eyebrow:** Why we started it

**H2:** The gap was always between the two vendors, not inside either one.

**Body:**
Justin had spent a decade studying how workplaces affect people's health, then built a
company running HR, payroll and absence management for Ontario small businesses. Alex had
spent those years building websites, search presence and the systems underneath them for
the same kind of company.

Working the same market from opposite ends, we kept arriving at the same finding from
different directions. The HR side would fix an onboarding process and then wait days on
whoever handled IT to actually create the accounts. The technology side would build a site
that generated leads for a business with no process to answer them. Neither problem was
inside either discipline. Both were in the seam between them, and the seam was exactly
where nobody was accountable.

That seam is what Vitops is for. When the same partner runs the joiner-mover-leaver process
and the identity system it writes into, onboarding isn't a handoff. When the same partner
runs the campaign and the CRM the lead lands in, nothing is anyone else's fault.

**Pull quote:**

> Every vendor we watched do this well did half of it, and then waited on someone else.

Justin holds a PhD in Population Health and spent more than a decade studying how

## Section 6 — What "one partner" means with two founders

**Eyebrow:** How the work is owned

**H2:** Two of us. One of us on your account.

**Lead:**
Worth being precise about, because "one point of contact" is a claim every provider makes
and most of them mean an account manager.

**Body:**
Each account has one of us on it as the owner — the person who makes the decisions, does
the work, and picks up when something breaks. Not a queue, and not someone relaying your
question to the person who actually knows.

What the second founder gives you is that the other half of the job doesn't become
somebody else's vendor. When your onboarding process needs to write into your identity
system, that's one conversation between two people who built the company together, not a
ticket between two firms who've never met.

The honest constraint: this doesn't scale indefinitely, so we take on a limited number of
accounts. If we're at capacity we'll say so rather than stretching and doing it badly.

## Section 7 — How we work

**Eyebrow:** How we work

**H2:** Four things we've decided, so you don't have to ask.

**We publish our prices.**
Per seat, on the [pricing page](/pricing). Not "contact us for a quote", which usually
means the number depends on what you look like you can pay.

**Month to month, 30 days' notice.**
No annual lock-in on either service. We'd rather earn it monthly than hold it
contractually.

**You own what we build.**
Websites on open standards, systems in your tenants, records in accounts with your name on
them. If you leave, you leave with everything, and we'll help you hand it over.

**We'll tell you not to buy things.**
The first pass on a new account usually finds duplicate tools and licences nobody has
opened in months. Saying so is a better first month than selling you something else.

## Section 8 — Fit

**Eyebrow:** Fit

**H2:** Who this works for, and who it doesn't.

**Works well for:**
Ontario owner-led businesses roughly between 5 and 150 people — past spreadsheets and the
owner-as-accidental-IT stage, not ready for an internal department. Trades and
contractors, real estate brokerages, legal and accounting firms, medical and dental
clinics: the four we know deeply enough to be useful in the first week.
[More on industries →](/industries)

**Not a good fit:**
Under about five employees, where the admin isn't yet heavy enough to be worth handing
over. Anyone wanting an enterprise brand campaign — a different discipline, and not ours.
Anyone needing desk-side hardware repair; we manage warranties and get replacements
enrolled, but somebody local is physically handling the device. And anyone who wants a
vendor rather than a partner. This works when we're in the operating decisions, not taking
tickets.
