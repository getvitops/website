**H1:** RMPG — up to $5,000 for retail technology, with one important catch.

**Direct answer (first paragraph):**
The Retail Modernization Project Grant is an Ontario program administered by the Ontario
Centre of Innovation through the Digital Competence Centre. **As of July 2026** it provides
a matched grant of up to **$5,000** to Ontario retailers with a physical location open to
the public, for technology such as point-of-sale systems, online payments, inventory
management, CRM, cybersecurity tools and AI tools. Eligibility requires **1–50 full-time
employees**, incorporation and operation for **at least one year**, and **at least $100,000**
in annual revenue before taxes. The catch: **you can apply to RMPG or [DMAP](/funding/dmap)
— not both.** Approval and amounts are decided by OCI, not Vitops.
[Source: Ontario Centre of Innovation](https://www.oc-innovation.ca/programs/digital-competence-centre/) · verified 2026-07-30

## The trade nobody explains

This is the most consequential thing on the page, so it goes near the top rather than the
bottom.

RMPG and DMAP are mutually exclusive. Taking the $5,000 closes the door on the $15,000
planning grant — and, because [TDP](/funding/tdp) requires a completed DMAP, on the
$50,000 implementation grant behind it as well.

**So the real comparison isn't $5,000 versus $15,000. It's $5,000 versus a path worth up
to $65,000.**

That doesn't make RMPG the wrong choice. If what you need is a point-of-sale system and
online payments, the money is straightforward, the scope is small, and the DMAP path would
be overkill for the project. But if there's a larger modernisation ahead of you in the next
couple of years, spending your eligibility on the small grant is a decision worth making
deliberately rather than by accident.

## FAQ

**Can we do RMPG now and DMAP later?**
No. They're alternatives, not stages.

**Does an online-only store qualify?**
No. A physical commercial location open to the public is required, and home-based
businesses are excluded.

**Do restaurants and salons count?**
Yes — the program covers direct-to-consumer businesses with a physical location, which
includes food service and personal services, not only shops.

**Is $5,000 worth the paperwork?**
For a defined project like a POS replacement, generally yes. As a reason to skip DMAP,
generally no.
