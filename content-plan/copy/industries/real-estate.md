## Section 2 — Direct answer

A brokerage's operational problem is that its people are neither quite employees nor quite
outsiders. Agents need company email, CRM access, listing tools and board-connected systems
on the day they sign, they take client relationships with them when they go, and the
commission arrangement behind all of it has to reconcile exactly. Standard employee
lifecycle tooling assumes a stability that brokerages don't have.

## Section 3 — What's actually different here

**Eyebrow:** What's different

**H2:** Three things brokerages get wrong, almost universally.

**Onboarding speed is a recruiting argument.**
An agent who signs on Monday and is still waiting for email on Thursday has already
started wondering. When a competing brokerage can have them productive the same afternoon,
provisioning speed is not an IT detail — it's part of why they chose you, or didn't.

**Departure is where the data leaks.**
An agent leaving takes their book with them by right. What they should not take is the
brokerage's CRM export, the shared drive, or continued access to the systems they used
last week. In practice most brokerages discover months later that at least one former
agent still has a live login, and the conversation that follows is unpleasant.

**Board tools don't integrate, and never will.**
Board-connected systems and MLS feeds were not designed to sit inside a modern stack, and
no amount of wishing changes that. The realistic goal is a clean, documented boundary — one
place data crosses, monitored — rather than the frictionless integration nobody can
actually build.

## Section 5 — FAQ

**H2:** Questions brokerages ask us.

**How quickly can a new agent be working?**
Same day, once the process is built. The build is a one-time exercise — mapping what an
agent actually needs, which is usually more systems than anyone remembers — after which
adding one is a form rather than a project.

**What happens to their access when an agent leaves?**
Revoked everywhere the same day, on a checklist that includes the systems people forget:
the shared drive, the marketing tools, the listing platforms, and any group inbox they
were on. We also flag what they're entitled to take, so the split is clean rather than
argued about later.

**Can you connect our board tools to our CRM?**
Sometimes, partially, and we'll tell you honestly which. Where a proper integration isn't
available we build a controlled, documented handoff instead of pretending otherwise —
that's a more reliable outcome than a fragile connector that breaks every time the board
updates something.

**Do you handle agent commission payouts?**
We handle the administration and the records — calculating against your splits, keeping
statements accurate, and making sure what's paid matches what's recorded. The financial
and tax treatment stays with your accountant.
