# /industries

**Title:** `Who we work with — Ontario small and mid-size businesses | Vitops`
**Description:** `Vitops works with Ontario businesses at the size where administration turns into a real cost. Trades, brokerages, professional firms and clinics are where we see it most.`
**Schema:** `Organization`, `BreadcrumbList`, `ItemList`

**Job of this page:** route, and qualify. Keep it short; the substance lives on the
four vertical pages.

**Two corrections shaped this page.**

_First:_ the original draft read as though Vitops serves **only** four industries. It
doesn't. Industry is a proxy, not a boundary — the four verticals are where the fit
concentrates, which is why we know their tools already, but a manufacturer or a studio in
the same position is the same engagement.

_Second:_ the replacement then over-corrected into targeting **owner-led** businesses —
"You were good at the work. Then you inherited a back office." That turned a common client
profile into the qualifier. Owner-led is something we observe, not something we screen on.

**The actual qualifier is a threshold, not an identity.** Past a certain size,
administration stops being absorbed into evenings and becomes a measurable draw on time
and money that would otherwise go to revenue work — typically half a day to two days a
week of someone's time, on work that earns nothing. That is stage and economics, and it
applies whoever owns the company.

Practical consequence: no standalone "if you're not one of these" section. That framing
concedes the exclusivity first and then walks it back. The inclusive framing goes up
front, and the four industries are introduced as examples from the outset.

## Section 1 — Hero

**Eyebrow:** Who we work with

**H1:** Past a certain size, the back office starts eating the work that pays.

**Lead:**
Administration never earned anything. For a while that's fine — it gets absorbed into
evenings and gaps between jobs. Then headcount, software and obligations grow faster than
the people handling them, and it turns into real hours and real money taken off the work
you actually get paid for.

## Section 2 — Direct answer

_Quotable standing alone — see `geo.md`._

Vitops works with Ontario businesses, typically between 5 and 150 people, that have
reached the size where back-office administration is a measurable cost rather than a
rounding error — usually somewhere between half a day and two days a week of someone's
time, spent on work that generates no revenue.

## Section 3 — The fit

**Eyebrow:** The fit

**H2:** What our clients have in common.

**Lead:**
This matters more than the sector. Many of our clients are owner-led and the founder is
still close to the work — but that's a pattern we notice, not a requirement. If these four
describe you, the name on the door is mostly a detail.

**Cards:**

**Nobody's actual job is the back office.**
It's split between people whose real work is something else — the owner, the office
manager, whoever had a gap. It gets done, but it gets done last, and it's nobody's
specialism.

**Admin competes directly with revenue work.**
Every hour on payroll, licences and paperwork is an hour not spent on what the business is
paid for. The maths gets worse as you grow, because the admin grows faster than the number
of people absorbing it.

**Past spreadsheets, short of a department.**
Usually somewhere between 5 and 150 people. Too big to keep running on memory and a shared
drive; too small to justify hiring for IT, HR and marketing separately.

**Something is regulated or confidential.**
Client files, patient records, safety documentation, licensing. Not always onerous, but
enough that getting access control wrong has consequences beyond inconvenience.

## Section 4 — The four

**Eyebrow:** Where it shows up most

**H2:** Four sectors where this happens early.

**Lead:**
We know these four well enough to be useful in the first week — the tools, the rules, and
what urgent means at four in the afternoon. If you're somewhere else, the service is the
same; it just takes us a little longer to learn your particulars, and we'll say so rather
than pretend otherwise.

**01 · Trades and contractors** → `/industries/trades`
Crews on sites, tablets that need to work without signal, a dispatch process that can't
wait for head office. Plus safety documentation that has to survive an inspection.

**02 · Real estate brokerages** → `/industries/real-estate`
A roster that turns over faster than anything else we work with, board-connected tools
that don't integrate cleanly, and commission administration that has to be exactly right.

**03 · Legal, accounting and professional firms** → `/industries/professional-services`
Matter-based permissions, conflict checks, and retention rules where the consequence of
getting access wrong is professional rather than merely awkward.

**04 · Medical and dental clinics** → `/industries/clinics`
PHIPA obligations, an EMR that dictates a lot of the environment, and front-desk staff who
cannot be waiting on IT while patients are in the room.
